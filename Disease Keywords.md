# Disease Keywords

Every genuine, substantive reference to disease, sickness, plague, or infirmity found across the chapter's 32 sections — not incidental one-word mentions, but places where illness functions as part of the actual argument or example. Built the same way as [Chapter Keywords](Chapter%20Keywords.md): from each section's full topic file, not its curated glossary.

The references aren't scattered at random. Five real patterns account for most of them:

1. **Aristotle and Aquinas's stock example for analogical predication.** "Healthy" said of an animal (intrinsically), its urine (a sign of health), and its medicine (a cause of health) is the single most-repeated illustration in the chapter — the cleanest case anyone found of a word meaning several genuinely different things without being pure accident or pure sameness. It anchors 1f, 2b, 3b, 3c(1), 3c(2), 3c(3), 3d, 4a, 4b, 4c(1), and 5f.
2. **Augustine's whole doctrine of signs is framed as medicine.** Humanity is a "sick soul," Scripture is "the medicine of Scriptures," and the Incarnation itself is Christ the physician treating sin "by contraries and by likes." Runs through 3c(1), 3c(2), 4c(1), and most fully 5e.
3. **Plague and healing as the paradigm divine sign** — the Exodus plagues, Christ's healing miracles, Hezekiah's illness, Job's boils, and the plague in the Greek camp read as Apollo's anger (cited independently by Homer, Herodotus, Hegel, and Aristotle). Concentrated in 5a, 5b, 5c, 5e, with an echo in 6a.
4. **Freud's literal clinical register** — not metaphor. Disease and illness are the actual subject matter throughout 6, 6a, 6b, and 6c.
5. **Medicine's unreliability as a model for skepticism about signs generally** — Montaigne (independently, in both 3a and 4e), Fielding's *Tom Jones* surgeon, and Pascal's *Provincial Letters* casuistry.
